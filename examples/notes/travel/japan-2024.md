# Japan, April 2024

Two weeks: Tokyo, Kyoto, Nara as a day trip, and a night in Osaka. The cherry blossoms were a week late, which worked out.

## Getting around

The 7-day rail pass got much more expensive after the price increase, and for a simple Tokyo to Kyoto to Osaka route it no longer paid for itself. Buying individual shinkansen tickets at the station machines was cheaper and easy. Reserve seats a day ahead in the busy weeks.

For local trains, metro and buses, put a Suica card in the phone wallet and top it up with a bank card. It works in convenience stores and vending machines too. I never had to buy a single paper ticket in the cities.

Luggage forwarding (takkyubin) from the hotel in Tokyo to the hotel in Kyoto cost about 2,500 yen per suitcase and arrived the next day. Riding the shinkansen with only a backpack was so much nicer.

## Kyoto

Fushimi Inari at 6:30 in the morning: almost empty, and the gates look best in the low light. By 9 it was shoulder to shoulder. Same story at the bamboo grove in Arashiyama, so go first thing or skip it.

Stayed in a machiya near Gion. The neighbourhood is lovely in the evening, but do not photograph people on the private lanes, there are signs and fines.

## Tokyo

- Shimokitazawa for second-hand clothes and coffee
- Yanaka for an older, quieter Tokyo, good for an afternoon walk
- teamLab Planets needs a booking weeks ahead
- the free observation deck at the government building in Shinjuku is as good as the paid towers

## Food

Convenience store breakfasts were genuinely good (egg sandwiches, onigiri). Best meal of the trip: a tiny standing sushi bar near the Tsukiji outer market at 7am. In Kyoto, the tofu restaurant near Nanzenji needs a reservation.

Many small restaurants only take cash, so keep 10,000 yen on you. Cash machines at the post office and at 7-Eleven accept foreign cards.

## Etiquette I want to remember

- no tipping anywhere; it comes across as awkward or even rude
- keep quiet on trains, no phone calls
- shoes off when you see a raised floor or slippers at the entrance
- carry your rubbish home, there are almost no public bins

## Next time

Fewer cities, more nights in each. A ryokan with an onsen in the mountains instead of a third night in Osaka.
