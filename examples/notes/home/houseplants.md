# Houseplants

What each plant actually wants, so I stop killing them.

## Watering

Check with a finger, not the calendar. Water when the top few centimetres of soil are dry, then water thoroughly until it drains out of the bottom, and empty the saucer after half an hour. Sitting in water rots roots faster than anything.

- Monstera: about once a week in summer, every two weeks in winter
- Snake plant: once a month at most, it thrives on neglect
- Pothos: when the leaves start to droop slightly, it perks up within hours
- Fiddle leaf fig: hates being moved, keep it by the east window and leave it alone

## Reading the leaves

Yellow lower leaves, especially with soggy soil, almost always mean too much water. Let it dry out properly before the next watering and check that the pot has a drainage hole.

Brown crispy edges usually mean the air is too dry or the plant sits in a cold draught. The radiator next to the bookshelf was the culprit for the calathea.

Pale, stretched growth leaning towards the window means it needs more light.

## Pests

Fungus gnats came back in October. They live in wet topsoil, so letting the soil dry out between waterings fixes most of it. Yellow sticky traps catch the adults.
