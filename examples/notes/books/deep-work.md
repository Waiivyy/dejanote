# Deep Work, by Cal Newport

My notes, in my own words.

## The core idea

Focused work on hard problems, without distraction, is getting rarer and more valuable at the same time. Shallow work (email, chat, meetings, admin) feels productive but is easy to replicate.

## Attention residue

When you switch from task A to task B, part of your attention stays stuck on A for a while. Checking chat "for a second" in the middle of a hard problem costs far more than the second.

## What I am trying

- two 90-minute blocks in the morning with notifications off and the phone in another room
- batching email and chat to three fixed times a day
- a shutdown ritual at the end of the day: review tomorrow's calendar, write down open loops, then stop thinking about work
- being bored on purpose: no phone in queues

## Disagreements

The book assumes you control your calendar. With on-call duty and a team to support, a fully scheduled deep work day is unrealistic for me. Two protected blocks is the realistic version.
