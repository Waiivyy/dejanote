# Sourdough starter

Notes on keeping the starter alive after the winter it nearly died. Its name is Clint.

## Feeding

Discard all but 50 g, then add 50 g flour and 50 g lukewarm water and stir hard. Twice a day when it lives on the counter, once a week when it lives in the fridge. A 1:1:1 ratio by weight keeps it predictable; 1:5:5 if I want it to peak overnight instead of in four hours.

Rye flour wakes it up fastest after a long stretch in the fridge.

## Signs it is ready to bake

- roughly doubled within 4 to 6 hours of a feed
- domed top, lots of bubbles at the sides of the jar
- smells tangy and a bit like yoghurt, not like nail polish
- a spoonful floats in water (not perfectly reliable, but a decent hint)

## When it goes wrong

Grey liquid on top ("hooch") means it is hungry, not dead. Pour it off and feed it. A nail polish smell is the same story: it ran out of food. Two feeds in a row usually fixes it.

Pink or orange streaks mean contamination. Throw it out and start over, do not try to rescue it.

## Before a holiday

Feed it, let it sit an hour, then into the fridge. It survived three weeks like this. For longer trips, smear a thin layer on baking paper, let it dry completely and crumble it into a jar. It rehydrates in about three days.
